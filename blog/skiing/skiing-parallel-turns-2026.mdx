## Why parallel turns matters in Skiing

Parallel turns come from matching the skis early and steering with the legs. Rushing the transition creates skidding, while a smooth release and edge change produce a controlled arc. Small technical gains compound quickly because better positions reduce wasted effort and lower the risk of injury.

Skiing places specific demands on the body and the decision-maker: sustained isometric leg work, balance under changing terrain, cold exposure, and rapid adjustments to snow, light, and crowds. This skill is where those demands meet technique, so improving it makes every surrounding skill easier to express.

## Technique Essentials

Anchor every session on these coaching principles:

- Start the turn by releasing the old edge and moving the hips across the skis.
- Match the inside ski to the outside ski early in the turn.
- Steer both legs through the turn rather than twisting the upper body.
- Keep the hands forward and the pole plant light.
- Finish the turn with the skis across the fall line before releasing.

## Drills to Build the Skill

- Snowplough-to-parallel — start in a snowplough and match the skis at the fall line.
- Garland drill — linked turns across a gentle slope with a focus on edge release.
- Thousand-step — lift and step the inside ski to feel weight transfer.
- Short-radius turns — make small parallel turns to develop quick edge changes.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Twisting the shoulders to start the turn instead of using the legs.
- Keeping the inside ski on the wrong edge and crossing the tips.
- Rushing the transition and skidding the tails.
- Copying an advanced athlete's routine without adapting it to your current capacity and schedule.
- Treating one good session as proof of mastery instead of testing the skill again under different conditions.

## Terminology

- **Fall line:** The most direct downhill path on a slope.
- **Edge angle:** The angle between the ski base and the snow, which controls grip and turn shape.
- **Carve:** A turn where the ski bends and grips through the arc with minimal skidding.
- **DIN:** The release setting on a binding, adjusted to the skier’s measurements and ability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I stop my skis from crossing?**  
Match the skis earlier and keep the inside ski slightly ahead. Crossing usually comes from late edge change.

**Do I need to ski parallel to have fun?**  
No. A controlled snowplough on the right terrain is perfectly enjoyable. Progress at your own pace.

**Do I need a coach to improve?**  
A coach accelerates feedback, but you can make real progress with video review, clear cues, and a simple training log. The key is comparing execution with a standard rather than guessing.

## Practical Coaching Cues

Keep these cues short enough to use in the middle of a session:

- **Prepare early:** start the movement before the pressure arrives, so parallel turns has room to work.
- **Stay relaxed:** grip, shoulders, and face should stay loose until the moment force is required.
- **Finish balanced:** if you cannot hold the landing or follow-through for two seconds, reduce the effort.
- **Use one cue:** pick a single word or image and repeat it for the whole set.
- **Stop while quality is high:** end the drill one or two repetitions before form starts to decay.
