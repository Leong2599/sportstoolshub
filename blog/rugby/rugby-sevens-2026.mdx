## Why sevens matters in Rugby

Rugby sevens rewards speed, decision-making, and recovery. With fewer players and more space, every missed tackle and slow support line is magnified. Small technical gains compound quickly because better positions reduce wasted effort and lower the risk of injury.

Rugby places specific demands on the body and the decision-maker: repeated collisions, sprinting, grappling, and changes of direction, plus the aerobic capacity to recover between bouts of high-intensity contact. This skill is where those demands meet technique, so improving it makes every surrounding skill easier to express.

## Technique Essentials

Anchor every session on these coaching principles:

- Attack with width and keep the ball alive through offloads.
- Defend with aggressive line speed and press the ball carrier.
- Recover quickly between matches with hydration and short rests.
- Use the kick as a space-gaining option when the attack is crowded.
- Prioritise repeat-sprint fitness over long-distance endurance.

6. **Review and progress.** Compare the session with your baseline, keep what worked, and change only one variable next time.

## Drills to Build the Skill

- Repeat-sprint sets — six 30-metre sprints with short recovery.
- Seven-on-seven touch — play touch rugby to develop width and support.
- Two-on-one finishing — attack space with a support runner.
- Kick-and-chase — practise contestable kicks and recovery.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Playing like fifteen-a-side and using too many forwards around the ball.
- Standing flat in defence and giving the attack easy outside space.
- Neglecting recovery between tournament matches.
- Copying an advanced athlete's routine without adapting it to your current capacity and schedule.
- Treating one good session as proof of mastery instead of testing the skill again under different conditions.

## Sample Training Week

Adapt this template to your level, available time, and competition calendar. The aim is quality, not exhaustion.

- **Day 1 — Technique:** Low-intensity repetitions with frequent feedback and full recovery.
- **Day 2 — Strength and mobility:** General strength plus targeted mobility for the positions the sport demands.
- **Day 3 — Skill application:** Drills that combine the focus with movement, decision-making, or a partner.
- **Day 4 — Recovery:** Easy movement, stretching, and sleep priority; no high-intensity work.
- **Day 5 — Pressure session:** Score-based or time-based practice that tests technique under fatigue.
- **Day 6 — Top-up:** Short, specific work on the weakest link plus light conditioning.
- **Day 7 — Rest:** Full day off to consolidate adaptation and restore motivation.

## Progress Checkpoints

Review these signals every two weeks:

- Your technique looks the same on video at the start and end of a set, even when fatigue rises.
- Your coach or training partner needs fewer corrections on the same cue.
- You can reproduce the skill in a scored or timed context without losing form.
- Soreness resolves within 24-48 hours and does not accumulate across the week.

## Equipment and Setup

Train at a rugby pitch, gym, or contact training area with qualified supervision. Check these items before the session starts:

- **Mouthguard:** choose the option that matches your current level and the surface you train on most.
- **Rugby boots:** keep it clean and stored correctly; neglect shortens useful life and hurts performance.
- **Headgear (optional):** prioritise comfort and safety over the newest model or marketing claim.
- **Training bibs:** test any change in a low-stakes session before using it in competition.
- **Tackle bags:** carry a simple backup for the item you rely on most.
- **Rugby ball:** check fit and condition before every session rather than replacing it on a hunch.

## Safety and Injury Prevention

Common issues in Rugby include concussion, shoulder dislocation, knee ligament injury, ankle sprain. Reduce risk with a few consistent habits:

- **concussion:** warm up thoroughly and include sport-specific preparation before high speed work.
- **shoulder dislocation:** respect pain that changes your movement; persistent symptoms deserve professional assessment.
- **knee ligament injury:** schedule recovery as deliberately as you schedule training.
- Keep a short log of volume, intensity, and symptoms so you can spot a trend before it becomes an injury.

## Terminology

- **Ruck:** A contest for the ball on the ground after a tackle, with players bound over the ball.
- **Maul:** A dynamic contest where players bind and drive while the ball is held off the ground.
- **Lineout:** A restart where forwards contest a thrown ball along the touchline.
- **Breakdown:** The phase immediately after contact when the ball must be secured or turned over.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long is a sevens match?**  
Usually two seven-minute halves with a short half-time. Tournament days can include several matches.

**What is the key fitness quality?**  
Repeat-sprint ability. Sevens is a series of high-speed efforts with incomplete recovery.

**Do I need a coach to improve?**  
A coach accelerates feedback, but you can make real progress with video review, clear cues, and a simple training log. The key is comparing execution with a standard rather than guessing.

## Practical Coaching Cues

Keep these cues short enough to use in the middle of a session:

- **Prepare early:** start the movement before the pressure arrives, so sevens has room to work.
- **Stay relaxed:** grip, shoulders, and face should stay loose until the moment force is required.
- **Finish balanced:** if you cannot hold the landing or follow-through for two seconds, reduce the effort.
- **Use one cue:** pick a single word or image and repeat it for the whole set.
- **Stop while quality is high:** end the drill one or two repetitions before form starts to decay.

## Recovery and Load Notes

- Give Rugby sessions at least 24 hours before repeating high-intensity work in the same pattern.
- Increase weekly volume by no more than roughly ten percent at a time.
- Use sleep, appetite, and morning soreness as simple recovery checkpoints.
- Schedule a lighter week every three to four weeks to consolidate adaptation.
